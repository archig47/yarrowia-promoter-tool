- 2026-09-30 — MULTI-CONDITION BENCHMARK, prompted by the question of whether a glucose-only label can distinguish a constitutively strong promoter from one that is merely glucose-induced. Rebuilt from all 6 Lubuta condition groups (2 strains x 3 carbon sources): 6,026 promoters with strength, consistency across carbon sources, and strain robustness. data/yarrowia/yarrowia_multicondition.json.
- 2026-09-30 — THREE FINDINGS FROM IT. (a) Averaging conditions improves strength prediction 0.390 -> 0.404, since it reduces label noise for free. (b) CONSISTENCY IS NOT SEQUENCE-PREDICTABLE: CV spearman +0.096, against +0.404 for strength and +0.246 for strain robustness. The sequence encodes how strong a promoter is but not how condition-responsive - sensible, since responsiveness depends on which TFs are active, which the promoter alone cannot say. (c) The feared failure mode is small in this data: no top-decile-strength promoter differs more than ~3x between glucose and glycerol, and the most consistent are flat to 2dp. Caveat: only 3 carbon sources, all steady-state chemostats.
- 2026-09-30 — TOOL DESIGN CONSEQUENCE: predict strength from sequence; REPORT measured consistency as an annotation rather than predicting it. For a novel sequence only strength is available, and the tool must say so.
- 2026-09-30 — CONTEXT FROM THE TARGET LAB: Patel et al. 2026 (Trends in Biotechnology, corresponding author Rodrigo Ledesma-Amaro) show that 19 Yeast Toolkit promoters vary in MACRO-HETEROGENEITY (discrete high/low producer subpopulations) and that swapping heterogeneous for homogeneous promoters raised lycopene yield threefold. They predict heterogeneity from scRNA-seq, not from sequence. This is a complementary axis this project does not cover and cannot, since no single-cell data exists for Yarrowia. It must be stated as a limitation: ranking by predicted mean strength can recommend a promoter that loses yield to heterogeneity.
- 2026-09-18 — MAJOR CORRECTION: THE BARRIER IS NOT THE SPECIES, IT IS THE ASSAY AND CHROMATIN CONTEXT. Built the control that had been missing: S. cerevisiae native promoters with an RNA-seq label (GSE316459 BY4741 wild-type, DESeq2-normalised, 5,484 promoters, 1000bp upstream from R64) - matched to the Yarrowia RNA-seq benchmark in label type and context, differing only in species. Zero-shot from the de Boer ridge model, 250bp window: S. cerevisiae RNA-seq +0.121, Yarrowia RNA-seq +0.075. Species contribution -0.046 (250bp) / +0.035 (80bp) - essentially ZERO. Against the in-assay reference of +0.864. So the model fails just as badly on its OWN species' native promoters as on Yarrowia's. Every earlier claim attributing the failure to a species barrier is wrong as stated.
- 2026-09-18 — CORRECTED DECOMPOSITION of the transfer ladder: held-out random 80mers 0.902; Native80 (native SEQUENCE, same pTpA scaffold, same FACS assay) 0.813, so sequence type costs only 0.09; S. cerevisiae native promoters in NATIVE chromatin with an RNA-seq readout 0.121, so context+assay costs 0.69; Yarrowia 0.075, so species adds a further 0.05. The dominant term by an order of magnitude is the assay and chromatin context, not evolution.
- 2026-09-18 — REFRAMES THE PROJECT'S CLAIM. Not 'cross-species transfer fails' but 'GPRA models trained on random inserts in a fixed scaffold do not predict native promoter activity, even within the same species'. That is a statement about the random-library paradigm itself, demonstrable in one species, and more consequential than an evolutionary-distance result. The RSC/chromatin mechanism from 09-16 still stands as the cause - RSC is a chromatin remodeller and the fixed-scaffold assay is dominated by nucleosome positioning - but it is a context effect, not a species effect.
- 2026-09-18 — UNEXPLAINED AND WORTH FLAGGING: Yarrowia native promoters are MORE predictable in-domain (CV rho 0.390) than S. cerevisiae native promoters (0.169), on matched pipelines. Candidate explanation: the Yarrowia data is chemostat steady-state (Lubuta et al.) while the S. cerevisiae data is batch culture (GSE316459), so the labels differ in noise. Not tested.
- 2026-09-18 — IMPLICATION FOR THE TOOL GOAL: do not use de Boer data to predict Yarrowia promoter strength. Train directly on the Yarrowia RNA-seq benchmark, which already reaches 0.390 in-domain - five times what any transfer route delivers.
- 2026-09-16 — WHY TRANSFER FAILS, PART 2: conserved signal EXISTS but is weak, and the models learn the non-conserved signal instead. All 243 usable de Boer/YeTFaSCo motifs scored by best PWM log-odds in both species and correlated with expression separately. The two effect vectors agree at spearman +0.468; 164/243 motifs share their sign; of the 145 strong in S. cerevisiae (|rho|>0.15), 109 (75%) keep their sign in Yarrowia. So regulatory grammar is PARTIALLY CONSERVED. But magnitudes collapse: the largest consistent Yarrowia effect is ~0.11 (ECM23, GAT4, ADR1, MIG3) while S. cerevisiae effects reach 0.52. Meanwhile the strongest S. cerevisiae motifs are INERT in Yarrowia - RSC3 +0.518 -> -0.021, PDR3 +0.396 -> -0.007, WAR1 +0.351 -> -0.002, PHD1_long +0.379 -> -0.002.
- 2026-09-16 — AND THOSE ARE EXACTLY THE MOTIFS D3 FOUND THE MODELS LEARNING. RSC30/RSC3 was the CNN's dominant ISM pattern (354 seqlets, r=0.948) and DNABERT-2 recovered WAR1 at r=0.96 and RSC3 at r=0.98. So the models preferentially learn the loudest features, which are the ones that do not transfer. Mechanistically plausible: RSC is a chromatin-remodelling complex and de Boer's assay puts random inserts in a fixed scaffold where nucleosome positioning dominates, whereas Yarrowia's native promoters sit in native chromatin. CONCLUSION: transfer fails not because nothing is transferable, but because the LEARNABLE signal and the TRANSFERABLE signal are different sets. This motivates suppressing the dominant non-transferable features rather than more transfer machinery.

- 2026-09-15 — WHY TRANSFER FAILS, with a mechanism rather than speculation. The two species' 6-mer coefficient vectors are ORTHOGONAL: pearson -0.0003, spearman +0.0033. The direct cause: GC content vs expression is spearman +0.604 in de Boer's random library but -0.032 in Yarrowia's native promoters. A large part of what the source models learned is 'GC-rich means strong', which is worth 0.60 in the source domain and nothing in the target. This quantitatively confirms a published concern that genomic models may rely on GC-content bias rather than functional regulatory grammar.
